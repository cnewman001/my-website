My Body, My Choice: How About No Choices?

By Vala Newman | Category: Cultural Critique & Feminism

Quick Summary

A deep dive into 3,600 years of bodily control, from Minoan corsets and corporate bra patents to nipple censorship and why taking back ownership of female bodies is necessary.

Men have had control over something involving our bodies since 1600 BCE. Let that speak.

From ancient Minoan stays to Victorian steel boned corsets and modern underwire, society has spent over three thousand years finding new ways to cage, shape, and dictate the female form. We were told corsets were for virtue and class, bras were for support, and modesty was for our own protection. But when you look past the history and the multi billion dollar marketing campaigns, the core message has never changed: your body does not belong to you.

The 3,600 Year Timeline of Control

Control over female bodies didn't start with modern dress codes or social media censorship; it was centuries in the making. In 1600 BCE, Minoan garments began lacing torsos tight. By the 16th century, male tailors and royal courts forced women into rigid, boned stays to flatten their bodies into artificial geometric cones. By the Victorian era, metal eyelets and steel bones were introduced, literally crushing ribs and shifting internal organs all to achieve an impossible "hourglass" standard. Women were deemed immoral or sloppy if they dared walk around unlaced.

When 19 year old Mary Phelps Jacob patented the first lightweight "Backless Brassiere" in 1913 using two silk handkerchiefs and ribbon, it was supposed to be a moment of liberation, a direct escape from the physical agony of the corset. Yet, men at the Warner Brothers Corset Company bought her patent for $1,500 and built a multi million dollar empire around it, turning a tool of freedom back into a system of control.

The Price of Admission and the Decency Trap

Historically, marriage was an economic transaction, and a woman's body was her bargaining chip. Conforming to painful standards, lacing into corsets that crushed internal organs was the tax paid to be deemed "appealing" to suitors.

Fast forward to today, and the physical tax remains. We've traded steel bones for underwires, but the underlying question persists: Who decided what is 'professional'?

Biologically, male and female nipples originate from the same tissue in the embryo. Yet, Western culture strictly categorized the female breast as a purely sexual object meant for public consumption while simultaneously demanding it be hidden to preserve "decency." In the 1930s, men protested and legally won the right to go topless in public. Women were left behind, locked into a hypocritical standard where a male nipple is treated as neutral, but an identical female nipple is censored, policed, and labeled "adult content."

Why are the very organs designed to nourish human life treated like contraband that must be locked up tight? Society hyper sexualizes our bodies for its own entertainment, yet shames us for existing in them naturally. Demanding a bra under the guise of 'decency' isn't about professionalism; it's about compliance.

The Backhanded Rights and Taking Back Control

We celebrate "progress" and boast about the rights we’ve been granted, but we turn a blind eye to the fine print. Every freedom came with conditions: you can work, but cover yourself; you can vote, but stay in line; you can exist, but only in a way that remains appealing and convenient to men.

We endured centuries of physical deformation, from steel corsets that crushed our internal organs to underwires that mark our skin, all to serve as acceptable transactions in a world that treated us like property. We were told to hide the very bodies that create and nourish human life.

"My body, my choice" isn't just a political chant for protest signs; it is a daily declaration of independence. It is the decision to stop apologizing for existing in our natural forms.

Without women, there is no lineage. There are no heirs, no labor force, no leaders, and no future. Society did not "grant" us our bodies; we are the foundation of existence itself. Taking back control means refusing to perform for a society that has spent over three millennia trying to regulate us. It is time to stop playing by the fine print, drop the shame, and take our power back.
