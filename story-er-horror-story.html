ER Horror Story

By Vala Newman | Category: Dark Fiction & Horror

Quick Summary

A bedridden patient in a chaotic emergency room forms a dark, twisted connection with his nurse, unleashing a chilling game of manipulation and murder.

When you arrive, it is the smell that hits you first. Hand sanitizer mixed with the stench of bodily fluids filling the hallways. You hear people screaming while watching nurses and EMTs drag gurneys through the halls, bumping into each other. A wall of beds where during a busy night you stay until a room becomes available. People in the rooms screaming for different things, nurses running around trying to help everyone. I remember the sound the X-ray machine made when they wheeled it past my room. Or how the lady next to me kept waking up saying "mom, mom," or the dude screaming "I'm hungry" over and over even after they just fed him. This is the ER on a typical night, but tonight was anything but typical.

I was rushed in on a stretcher one Thursday night. The weather was warm but windy. I don’t remember exactly how I got here, but I can hear the faint sounds of the EMTs. Someone is coding. Is it me? "And clear bzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzz," you hear the defibrillator followed by a soft thud back on the gurney. "Welcome back, you gave us quite a fright. Do you know your name, where you are, and what happened to you?" the female EMT asked. I believe she is talking to me, but I can’t open my mouth to speak. "Mrmrmrmmrmrmmr," was the only thing I managed to get out. "Well, he is responsive, that is one thing," the male EMT said. I drifted in and out of sleep, and when I opened up my eyes, suddenly I was in a room with blue and white walls. I look around and my eyes lay upon a beautiful sight. She had golden locks, blue eyes with little flecks of green, and a smile where her left dimple popped out. "Welcome back Mr. Doe, we have been waiting for you to get up. I’m Alexis, I will be your nurse for the day." I look over at her and smile weakly. "Water." Pointing to the tray next to me, I struggle to grab the water. "Here, let me help you with that." She brings the water over to my dried lips and I try really hard to swallow the smooth crisp feeling of the water going down my throat. I smile very slightly, "Thank you ma'am."

Over the next few weeks Alexis and I got acquainted. She took care of me as I healed, still confused on how I ended up here, but assuming my memories would come back. One day she looked at me and said, "I don’t know what it is, but I feel like I have known you forever. It is like kismet." And right then I knew she was perfect. So I smiled slightly and asked, "Do you like seeing people hurt?" She turns and suddenly gets this wicked smile, "You mean how when the EMTs brought you in all mangled and lifeless? I saw you code a few times and it gave me this rush." She starts to walk away. "Can I let you in on a secret?" I start, "I have killed a few people in my time." She stops suddenly and turns around. "How did you do it?" she asks as she walks back towards the bed. "I am sure you have read about couples tied together with a bow through their hearts. I like to think of myself as anti cupid. That newspaper didn’t tell the whole story," I state as I start to trail back off, but she looks as if she is eager to hear more, so I continue. "He was having an affair with his secretary and she was about to go down the same path with his best friend. In a way, I save the couples from themselves." She starts to laugh and says something I never would have thought I would hear anyone say: "I can understand the feeling of saving someone from themselves."

That confession opened the floodgates. Over the next few days, Alexis was not just nursing me back to health. She was becoming my hands.

It started with the guy down the hall, the one screaming he was hungry even after being fed, driving the staff insane. "He doesn't appreciate life," I whispered to her one evening, my voice smooth and deliberate. "People like him just waste space. You hold all the power here, Alexis. You could save everyone the trouble."

Her breath hitched. The next morning, his room went quiet. Cause of death was sudden cardiac arrest from a lethal dose of potassium chloride. But she didn't just stop at the injection. Tucked neatly around the drip line was a tiny crimson ribbon tied into a delicate bow, my exact signature.

She slipped into my room right after, her face flushed and her eyes wide with an electric thrill. "It gave me such a rush," she breathed, her left dimple popping as she leaned close. "I felt like an angel."

"You are an angel," I said, stroking her cheek. "An angel of mercy."

From that night on, the ER became her personal hunting ground. Patient after patient coded under mysterious circumstances, each left with the ritualistic bow. News of the Hospital Cupid broke nationwide, linking the fresh clinic murders to my own unsolved cold cases from before my accident.

While detectives swarmed the building, I sat propped up in bed with a bulletproof alibi. How could a bedridden amnesiac be committing murders right under the staff's nose?

"They're closing in, Alexis," I feigned panic a week later, clutching her trembling hands. "If they search the unit, they will find the supplies. Take my duffel bag to your apartment. Keep it safe for us. If they trap us, tell them it was all you. Save me, Alexis. Save our love."

Her eyes filled with a tragic, fanatic devotion. "I'll protect you," she whispered. "We are bound together forever."

The raid happened two days later. The police found my original murder trophies hidden in the duffel under her bed, alongside stolen hospital syringes and spools of red ribbon in her locker.

I watched through my room's glass window as they led her away in cuffs. She turned her head, looking back at me with absolute, unwavering worship. She didn't say a word to the investigators. She was taking the fall, completely convinced she was sacrificing herself for her soulmate.

I gave her a soft, mournful wave as the elevator doors closed.

A week later, my discharge papers were signed. My memory was fully intact, my injuries healed, and my slate wiped clean by the court of public opinion.

I stepped out of the ER into the warm, windy night, inhaling the crisp air. I caught a bus three towns over and walked into a dimly lit, 24 hour diner.

The waitress slammed a coffee mug onto my table, muttering a bitter insult under her breath about late night customers before violently wiping down the counter. She had dark hair, sharp eyes, and a flick of genuine cruelty in her sneer as she watched an elderly customer struggle with his wallet in the corner.

I took a slow sip of black coffee, smiled weakly, and leaned across the counter.

“Do you like seeing people hurt?”
