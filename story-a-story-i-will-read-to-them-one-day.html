A Story I Will Read to Them One Day

By Vala Newman | Category: Short Fiction & Creative Prose

Quick Summary

An intimate reflection on unspoken words, healing from past relationships, finding self love, and the quiet courage it takes to trust someone new.

Unspoken

I fell in love with you, but I never said it. I didn’t want you running away.

There are so many things I want to say, but I know you don’t like that stuff. So I put it in writing. I could write you paragraphs on loving you. I’m writing poems, or maybe this book. There are so many things I want to say, but I can’t find the words to speak them out loud, so I write them down instead.

These are all the things I wish I could tell you in person:

I love how your hand fits in mine so perfectly. You would think they were made for each other. I want to kiss you all day. Your lips feel soft, and we move like we are in harmony. Our lips tell a story while our tongues dance around each other fighting for dominance.

I want to tell you how I love you all the time because saying it once isn't enough. I want you to know I love you every second of every minute of every hour. I want to be with you for a lifetime, or even many lifetimes, if you believe in that sort of thing.

I didn’t think someone could actually like me. You know I have my issues, and I always thought you were out of my league. If we had met in high school, you would have been popular and I would still be me, so we wouldn't have noticed each other. In a way, we compliment each other like bananas and peanut butter. Hearing them paired like that, you wouldn’t think they go together. But when you have them at the same time, you can tell they were made for each other.

You

When I look into your eyes, I can see everything.

I see our future, our life, and the woman I want to be. I can see myself becoming a better person because of you. I can see how you view me when I look in your eyes. When you compliment me, I feel transcendent.

One Day

One day we will be in love. One day we may start a family. One day I will look into your eyes and say I do.

But that is one day. Today we are young. Today we are still in love, and I still look into your eyes and say I do. That future is for one day, but for now, I am here present. Right now, I am in love.

June 26, 2019

This was the day everything began for us. It was the first day of a longtime relationship I believed we should have had.

I saw you differently. Your height was like a tree I would lay under to read a book. You were like a book I would have picked up and never put down again. If I could take one thing to a deserted island, I would have picked you.

But like every book, our love ended. It became toxic. You turned into my Kryptonite, a weakness I had to get rid of.

Time

As time went on, I healed in more ways than one. My heart became whole again. My smile grew brighter. The scars faded into the back of my mind.

Though there are times where I remember the hurt and how I cried to you, you are now a distant memory in a love I once loved. So this is a goodbye to you, and the start of something new.

Self

On this journey I am going through, I have learned to love the things I used to hate.

Like my hair. I have the curls of ocean waves, and I used to hide them behind flat irons. Or like the way my stomach slightly hangs when I sit down. I used to think gaining weight was a bad thing, but when you are happy, it shows. When I smile, I have a tiny dimple that shows. I always hated my smile.

Now, I am learning that every part of me is beautiful, and that I deserve the self love I am learning to build.

Relearning

I am relearning how to love the right way. Learning that means I have to unlearn the toxic traits and coping mechanisms I developed in past relationships.

Communication is key. I am learning to speak up if something bothers me, and to stop apologizing for everything because not everything is my fault. When you spend years hearing that it is all your fault and that you are to blame, it is difficult to hear that you did nothing wrong.

But for you, I am trying, because I think we can work.

Crossroads

I am at the stage where I am ready to love again, but I do not wish to go through that hurt again. It took 400 days to heal from that pain. I do not have another 400 days if the next person decides to hurt me, or decides that I am not good enough, or that I am just too broken to be given a chance.

To be honest, I don’t know how to love healthily yet. I have only seen the negative side. The yelling, the hitting, and the manipulations. The flowers handed over for "I'm so sorry, I won't do it again," just to turn around and do it again.

But there is a chance I might get the right side of love this time. The understanding, and someone being there just because. The "hey, I am going to be busy today, I don't want you overthinking" texts. The compassion. Maybe it just might turn into a lifelong dance.

You (Part 2)

Here we are again on the path of a possible love. My heart is guarded because I want to let you in, but I am afraid of it turning out bad. I am afraid I will give my heart to you just for you to smash it, letting my walls down just long enough to see if you are trustworthy.

My heart is already on its last threads, being held together by that small hope. The movie hope. The hopeless romantic hope. The fell at first sight love.

I want you, but only if you choose me back.

Trying Again

I was told I could trust you. I am not really good at expressing my feelings, and you aren't either. I remember you told me that. But when I talk to you, I feel like I can tell you anything.

I can tell you I was abused once before in a relationship. Or how I hate confrontations because I was once yelled at about not wanting to lay naked. Or how now I don't feel comfortable laying naked because I always had to be naked around my ex.

I can tell you how my birth mom left me as a kid, and how the mom who raised me got sick when I was eight years old. She didn't want me to see her get as bad as she did, so she sent me to my aunt's house. How I have never had a solid home after the age of eight because I always bounced around.

Eventually, everyone gets tired of me. It feels like a cycle. But with you, I am trying again.
