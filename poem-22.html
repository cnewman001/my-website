      <h1 class="poem-title">The Roommate</h1>

      <p class="poem-author">By Vala Newman</p>


      <div class="poem-body">

        <p>

          I’ve struggled to be happy<br>

          my whole life.<br>

          I don’t know why—<br>

          it’s just always been this way.

        </p>


        <p>

          Lately,<br>

          I’m no longer quietly not okay.<br>

          Now the sadness shows its teeth.

        </p>


        <p>

          When I’m sad,<br>

          I’m told I want attention.<br>

          When I don’t speak,<br>

          my silence becomes permission for excuses.

        </p>


        <p>

          I am taught rules for grief:<br>

          I can’t be sad because others have it worse.<br>

          I can’t be sad because I never said anything.<br>

          I can’t be sad because my sadness is inconvenient.

        </p>


        <p>

          But if you were paying attention,<br>

          you would’ve seen the child<br>

          sent to her room like a princess in a tower—<br>

          not protected,<br>

          just contained.

        </p>


        <p>

          Then my mother got sick,<br>

          and the tower disappeared.

        </p>


        <p>

          So I hid elsewhere.<br>

          In books.<br>

          In being useful.<br>

          In making myself smaller<br>

          so I could be loved without complaint.

        </p>


        <p>

          I learned to apologize<br>

          for existing too loudly,<br>

          too honestly,<br>

          too much.

        </p>


        <p>

          Growing up,<br>

          I learned how to hurt myself quietly.<br>

          Not with blades—<br>

          with bruises I could blame on sleep.<br>

          With hands against my own face,<br>

          just to feel something other than this<br>

          endless, echoing ache.

        </p>


        <p>

          People I loved passed on,<br>

          the ones who made life feel survivable.<br>

          And somehow,<br>

          I stayed.

        </p>


        <p>

          I traded pain for substances,<br>

          weed, alcohol, mushrooms—<br>

          because happiness found me there.<br>

          Laughter without permission.<br>

          A brighter world, briefly believable.

        </p>


        <p>

          But the world is dark.<br>

          People get sad.<br>

          People learn to cope.

        </p>


        <p>

          And now—<br>

          I’ve stopped hiding.<br>

          No substances.<br>

          No locked rooms.<br>

          No violence turned inward.

        </p>


        <p>

          Just me,<br>

          finally sitting with the sadness.

        </p>


        <p>

          I’ve lived through more than what is considered normal.<br>

          Most people would have shattered.<br>

          I bent.

        </p>


        <p>

          I am not perfect.<br>

          But I am strong enough<br>

          to live with my sadness<br>

          like a roommate—<br>

          the twin I never asked for,<br>

          never wanted,<br>

          but learned to love anyway.

        </p>
