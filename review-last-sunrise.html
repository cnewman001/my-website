Love Worth Forgetting: Why The Last Sunrise Brought Me to Tears

By Vala Newman | Category: Cinema & Movie Reviews

Quick Summary

Based on the novel by After author Anna Todd, The Last Sunrise follows Oriah "Ry" Pera (Maia Reficco), a young stage dancer from Texas battling Tuberous Sclerosis Complex (TSC) and severe epilepsy. When her overprotective mother, Isolde (Eva Longoria), travels to Mallorca, Spain to lead a luxury real estate development, Ry joins her to explore her roots. There, she falls for local fisherman Julián (Fernando Lindez) unaware that her mother's resort project threatens his family's generational docks, or that a life saving surgery will change everything.

The Prime Original movie adaptation of The Last Sunrise completely wrecked me in the best way possible. I was in literal tears by the time the credits rolled.

Family Guilt & Corporate Turnaround

What made this story hit so much deeper was that it was not just a simple summer romance; it was packed with heavy family drama and high stakes choices.

Ry's mother, Isolde, represents Redwyn, a development company attempting to acquire the Garcia family's centuries old waterfront docks. Meanwhile, Julián is carrying the weight of keeping his father Mateo's traditional fishing business alive. Ry finds herself caught right in the middle, falling in love with Julián while her own mother spearheads the deal that could erase his family's livelihood.

The personal history between the parents makes the stakes even higher. Isolde fled Mallorca as a young woman after her own mother died from TSC, leaving behind her first true love, Mateo. Mateo was the only person who called her "Izzy." The moment Julián hears that nickname, everything clicks, and he hands over all the unread love letters his father kept for years. That revelation changes everything: instead of destroying the docks, Isolde uses Redwyn’s resources to invest directly in the Garcia family business, saving their livelihood and honoring their shared history.

Numbness vs. Living: The Medical Crisis

Ry's struggle with her health felt so raw. Living with Tuberous Sclerosis Complex and severe epilepsy, she was trapped between taking heavy medications that numbed her completely just to stay safe, or risking a brain surgery that could erase her memory entirely.

Isolde's terror and extreme protectiveness over Ry stem from a secret guilt because she never told Ry that her grandmother died from the exact same genetic condition. That tension boils over in a massive fight between them about Ry wanting to actually live life instead of existing in a fog. Shortly after, Ry suffers a severe seizure, falls into the hotel pool, and stops breathing.

The Ultimate Act of Selfless Love

When Ry is hospitalized in critical condition, it is actually Julián who convinces Isolde to authorize the surgery. Knowing how terrified Ry was of losing her past, Julián selflessly tells Isolde that if the surgery saves her life, her favorite memories will be the ones ahead of her, even if that means she completely forgets him. To love someone so deeply that you would willingly become a stranger just so they can survive is pure romance angst at its absolute finest.

An Ending That Broke (and Rebuilt) Me

The movie handles the ending beautifully. After surviving the surgery, Ry begins recovering her life, though her past memories are hazy. Rather than walking away, Julián has the courage to step right back into her world at the docks, introduce himself for the first time all over again, and let fate take its course as she starts to fall for him a second time.

Final Verdict

With deep family history, corporate turnarounds, unread love letters, and high stakes emotional angst, The Last Sunrise is an absolute must watch.

Rating: 5/5 Stars
